An AI reading the answer another AI generated, to say whether it actually
answers the question. That's what I brought back from TDC in Florianópolis.

I was there on July 24th, with a ticket my tech lead Giovani Valgas got me —
thanks, Giovani. The schedule was dominated by AI: AI First, harness, model
evaluation.

Livia Rodrigues Fernandes Silva, from Oracle, gave a talk on AI as a Judge: one
model reads another's output and evaluates whether it answers the question and
respects the guardrails. The idea is simple; the value is in doing it
systematically. Given how many support chatbots are running today with nobody
looking at what they reply, that's a lot of return for very little engineering.

Harness was the other term that came up all day: everything orbiting the LLM
without being the LLM — plugins, MCPs, skills, the tooling around it.

I also took Ariane Izac's observability workshop. Hands-on: we instrumented an
e-commerce app inside DataDog's own course platform, with traces, dashboards and
alerts we configured ourselves.

What repeated was the speech about the value of the developer. Every speaker
said the same sentence in different words: the developer is a conductor now.
Except that sentence isn't new. Ten years ago it was "keep up or fall behind",
and the object was frameworks and architecture. Today the object is AI. The noun
changed, not the argument.
