A route parameter that accepted `/` was enough to reach every patient record on
the server.

That happened live at CodeCon, in Curitiba, on August 14th and 15th, during a
Capture the Flag run by YuriRDev. The target was an appointment scheduling system.

The path was simpler than I expected. The path param sanitized neither slashes
nor `../`, so the application started reading whatever the attacker typed as a
directory separator. The intended routing broke, and what was sitting on the
server became browsable — all the records, not one.

What got me wasn't the technique. It was realizing that the missing validation
is the same kind I write every day without thinking twice: take the id from the
route, use the id. I left with a question I still can't answer — which of my
routes accept a `/` nobody planned for.

I've always studied development best practices, the do-it-right side. I never
studied the inverse: how someone breaks that on purpose. Those are two different
disciplines, and it took watching the CTF to see how far apart they are. Next
step is being able to reproduce what he did, not just having watched it.
